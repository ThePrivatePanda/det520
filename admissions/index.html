SECTION 02 // ADMISSIONS

Joining Detachment 520

Enroll as a full-time student at one of our five campuses, register for our classes, and you are training with us. Scholarships, stipends, and a commissioned career follow for those who stay the course.

01 // HOW TO JOIN

Three steps to your first LLAB

STEP 1

Enroll at a partner school

Be a full-time student at Cornell University, Ithaca College, Binghamton University, SUNY Cortland, or Elmira College.

STEP 2

Register for our classes

Sign up for the Aerospace Studies academic class and Leadership Laboratory for your year. All classes meet in Barton Hall.

STEP 3

Create a WINGS account

Upload your documentation to become an active cadet. The cadre will guide you through every form.

Contact the cadre Eligibility standards at afrotc.com

AFROTC maintains medical, physical fitness, behavioral, and academic standards for admission and retention. Review them at the link above, or ask us — eligibility questions are what we are here for.

02 // SCHOLARSHIPS & PAY

What the Air Force invests in you

While in the program

  • Full-tuition high school scholarships for entering freshmen
  • In-college scholarships — full tuition for technical majors, up to $18,000/year for non-technical majors
  • Charles McGee Leadership Award — $18,000 per year after Field Training
  • $300–$500 nontaxable monthly stipend (on scholarship or in the POC)
  • $900 annual book allowance; uniforms provided
  • Language immersion, travel, base visits, and summer internships

As a commissioned officer

  • Competitive salary with 30 days of paid vacation per year
  • Medical care at no cost; tax-free shopping on installations
  • Tuition aid for graduate education
  • Retirement pension based on grade and years of service
  • Promotions based on initial appointment, grade, and experience
  • On-base amenities at reduced rates: pools, bowling, golf, tennis, officers' club, movie theaters
  • Space-available travel on military aircraft
  • VA benefits: GI Bill, home loans, life insurance

Service commitment after commissioning: four years as a line officer; six years for combat systems officers, air battle managers, and RPA pilots; ten years for pilots of manned aircraft.

04 // QUESTIONS, ANSWERED

Frequently asked

How do I join ROTC?

Be a full-time student at one of our supported universities and register for our classes — those two steps start your participation. You will also create a WINGS account and upload documentation to become an active cadet. We guide you through the whole process.

Which universities do you accept students from?

Binghamton University, Cornell University, Elmira College, Ithaca College, and SUNY Cortland.

What classes do I register for, and when are they?

All classes are located within Barton Hall. If you join as a sophomore, you take both the freshman and sophomore academic classes, but only the sophomore-level leadership lab. Junior and senior classes increase to three credit hours and meet twice a week.

How often do crosstown students commute to Cornell?

Generally once a week, on Thursdays. Monday physical training is only required for Cornell and Ithaca College students. ROTC does not provide transportation, but most crosstown cadets carpool.

What does parking look like for crosstown cadets?

Cornell operates daily-decision lots that require no semester pass. Most cadets park in Hoy Garage (lot 4121), just southeast of Barton Hall — use the lowest level, outside the gated area. Details and prices are at Cornell Transportation.

I'm past freshman year. Can I still make ROTC work?

The minimum track is three years. That rarely fits a junior or senior schedule on its own, but students can extend with graduate coursework to meet the requirement. Reach out if you are considering this route.

I'm in the Guard or Reserves. Can I do ROTC?

Yes — as long as your contract ends before you are due to commission. Contact us with the specifics of your situation.

Can I commission into the Guard or Reserves?

It is possible under certain circumstances, though the vast majority of our cadets commission directly into active duty. Reach out to learn more.

How welcoming is Cornell to the military and ROTC?

Very. Military training was written into Cornell's charter as a land-grant institution. The programs have their own building in the heart of campus, strong administrative support, and a growing veteran community. See military.cornell.edu.
