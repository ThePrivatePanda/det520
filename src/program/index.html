SECTION 01 // THE PROGRAM

The four-year program

AFROTC is split into two halves: the General Military Course, where you decide if the Air Force is for you, and the Professional Officer Course, where the Air Force invests in you as a future officer.

GMC // YEARS 1–2

General Military Course

A two-year program for freshmen and sophomores: one credit hour of Aerospace Studies each semester, two hours of Leadership Laboratory, and up to two hours of group physical training per week. The GMC builds communication skills and introduces military life.

Try before you commit — cadets not on scholarship carry no service obligation during the GMC. To enter the POC, cadets compete for an Enrollment Allocation and complete Field Training the summer between sophomore and junior year.

POC // YEARS 3–4

Professional Officer Course

Juniors and seniors who have completed Field Training sign a service contract and enter the POC. Coursework deepens to three credit hours per semester, and POC cadets plan and lead the detachment's training — running the cadet wing that the GMC classes train within.

Leadership, practiced daily — the POC is a two-year laboratory in leading people, with the cadre as coaches.

03 // THE CLASSROOM, YEAR BY YEAR

What you study

Each year pairs an Aerospace Studies course with Leadership Laboratory. The academics build from an introduction to the Air Force toward the judgment expected of a commissioned officer.

AIRS 1101 / 1102AS 100 · Freshman

Foundations of the Air Force

The Department of the Air Force, professionalism & security

A survey course introducing the basic characteristics, missions, and organization of the United States Air Force, alongside officership, military customs and courtesies, officer career opportunities, and an introduction to communication skills. Enrollment carries no obligation to join — but cadets who do join take this course together with Leadership Laboratory.

AIRS 2201 / 2202AS 200 · Sophomore

Team and Leadership Fundamentals

Preparing for Field Training

A fundamental grounding in leadership and team building, structured to prepare cadets for Field Training and for leadership positions within the detachment. Leadership Laboratory runs alongside it, adding the followership and teamwork experience that Field Training demands.

AIRS 3301 / 3302AS 300 · Junior

Leading People & Effective Communication

Leadership in a supervised environment

Drawing on each cadet's Field Training experience, the junior course takes a deeper look at leadership with special emphasis on communication. Cadets practice leadership and management techniques in a supervised environment and, through concurrent Leadership Laboratory, help plan, run, and teach the lab itself.

AIRS 4401 / 4402AS 400 · Senior

National Security & Commissioning Preparation

Ready for the first active-duty assignment

The senior course prepares cadets for their first assignment as an officer: the role of a professional military leader in a democratic society, the national security process, regional studies, advanced leadership ethics, and the application of Air Force doctrine in current conflicts. In tandem, seniors plan, organize, and facilitate the training of the entire cadet wing.

LLABAll years

Leadership Laboratory

The cadet-run laboratory

Taken every year alongside the Aerospace Studies class, Leadership Laboratory is run by the cadets themselves. Juniors and seniors exercise the leadership, managerial, and organizational skills they are learning; freshmen and sophomores get an immersion into the Air Force way of life through customs and courtesies, drill and ceremonies, physical fitness, and teamwork — the groundwork for Field Training and for leading the cadet corps.

04 // FIELD TRAINING

The proving ground

Field Training is a two-week training and evaluation period at Maxwell Air Force Base, Alabama, held between a cadet's sophomore and junior years. It challenges leadership, followership, and teamwork across two settings — in garrison and in the field — and is required to enter the Professional Officer Course.

Earning the seat. To attend, GMC cadets first compete for an Enrollment Allocation, awarded on qualitative factors: college GPA, unit commander evaluation, physical fitness scores, and aptitude test scores. On successful completion of Field Training, cadets enter the POC and assume greater leadership within the Cadet Wing.

The Field Training curriculum

  • Physical conditioning
  • Marksmanship training
  • Survival training
  • Air Force specialty orientation
  • Confidence courses
  • Group leadership projects
  • Human relations
  • Drill and ceremonies
  • Leadership study
  • Expeditionary leadership
