SECTION 05 // HERITAGE

Since before the Air Force existed

Cornellians have commissioned into every major conflict since the Spanish–American War. Detachment 520 carries a lineage older than the service it feeds.

01 // BARTON HALL

Our headquarters was a hangar

The building where today's cadets take class and drill — Barton Hall — housed military aircraft during the First World War. It is named for Colonel Frank A. Barton, Class of 1891, the first Cornellian granted a commission and twice Cornell's Professor of Military Science and Tactics.

Military instruction is written into Cornell's founding charter as a land-grant institution — and today's cadets and midshipmen are an integral part of fulfilling founder Ezra Cornell's dream of "an institution where any person can get instruction in any study."

More on Cornell's military history is kept on the Army ROTC Excelsior Battalion's website and the Veterans Memorials website created by the Cornell University Veterans Colleague Network Group.

02 // THE RECORD

A century of firsts

  1. 1891

    The first commission

    Frank A. Barton, Class of 1891, becomes the first Cornellian granted a commission, beginning a distinguished U.S. Army career.

  2. 1917

    First under fire

    A group of Cornell volunteers becomes the first U.S. unit in combat under fire in the First World War. Cornell commissions more officers in WWI than any other single institution.

  3. 1940

    Barton Hall named

    The New York State Armory and Drill Hall is renamed in Colonel Barton's honor, recognizing his influence on military training at Cornell.

  4. 1941–45

    The arsenal of officers

    Cornell inducts more officers and service members in World War II than any other single institution.

  5. 1947

    An air arm at Cornell

    As the United States Air Force is established as a separate service, Army ROTC at Cornell founds the Air Sciences and Tactics Division.

  6. 1950

    Detachment 520 activated

    The division formally becomes Air Force ROTC Detachment 520 under the command of Colonel George T. Crowell. Cadets have commissioned through it every year since.

03 // DISTINGUISHED ALUMNI

The company you join

CLASS OF '51

Col (Ret) Frederick Austin Crow Jr.

A teenager when he witnessed the attacks on Pearl Harbor, he enlisted in the Aviation Cadet Program two years later. After Cornell he flew as a fighter pilot and forward air controller; shot down in his F-4 Phantom II over Vietnam in 1967, he endured 2,171 days as a prisoner of war before his release in Operation Homecoming, 1973. He returned to speak with Det 520 cadets on November 11, 2015.

CLASS OF '56

The Hon. Thomas C. Reed

Cadet Wing Commander of Det 520 in the mid-1950s. Commissioned in 1956 with a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering, he served as the 11th Secretary of the Air Force and the 6th Director of the National Reconnaissance Office under presidents Ford, Carter, and Reagan. He returned as guest speaker at the detachment's Fall 2014 Dining Out.

CLASS OF '68

Maj Gen Michael Hall

Twenty-seven years of service and over 4,500 hours in fighters from the F-106 to the F-16. He commanded a Tactical Fighter Wing, pioneered F-16 close air support, and served as Senior Allied Air Liaison Officer in the first Gulf War. A continued supporter of the detachment, he has spoken at the military ball and the Spring 2016 commissioning ceremony.
